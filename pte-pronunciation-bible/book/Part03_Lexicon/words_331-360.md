# Words 331–360（词汇 331–360）

Part: Part III — PTE High Frequency Lexicon
Status: Draft (second half of Batch 9, combined with `words_301-330.md` to form a 60-word batch — see `README.md`)
Accent model: General American (GA), consistent with Parts II and IV

**Sourcing note**: same as prior batches — IPA and stress are standard, well-established dictionary pronunciations; this session's direct Cambridge/Oxford fetch attempts returned 403, so each entry should be individually cross-checked before being marked Done. "AI Recognition Notes" fields are `[Hypothesis]`-tagged reasoned extensions of Parts I and IV, not observed word-specific AI behavior data.

**Batch theme**: arts/literature (*literature, novel, poetry, author, narrative, fiction, character, theater, performance, exhibition, gallery, sculpture, architecture*), sports (*athlete, championship, tournament, stadium, spectator*), and travel/leisure (*leisure, hobby, recreation, tourism, destination, accommodation, itinerary, souvenir*).

**New pattern — first /ʒ/ example**: *leisure* contains /ʒ/ (the voiced postalveolar fricative, as in the middle of "vision"), a sound not previously featured in this lexicon. Mandarin lacks a direct phonemic equivalent; a plausible substitution is devoicing to /ʃ/ or approximating with /r/-like or /dʒ/-like sounds, but this specific pattern is not directly documented in this book's Part IV sources, so it is flagged as a cautious `[Hypothesis]` extension rather than an established mistake.

**Continuing patterns**: *character* and *architecture*/*architect* add a sixth and seventh spelling-mismatch example — Greek-origin "ch" pronounced /k/, not /tʃ/ — joining *process* (Batch 2), *knowledge* (Batch 3), *vehicle*, *psychology* (Batch 7). *athlete*→*athletic* is a fourth "-ic" family shift pair. *championship* confirms "-ship" is stress-neutral (alongside *citizenship*, companion file). *souvenir* is a second French-origin final-stress loanword, alongside *entrepreneur* (Batch 8). *exhibition* highlights English "x" spelling ambiguity (/ɡz/ here, vs /ks/ elsewhere) as a related but distinct spelling-inconsistency case.

Each entry follows `book/_TEMPLATE_LEXICON_ENTRY.md`. Chinese Learner Errors cross-reference Part IV's numbered mistakes where applicable.
