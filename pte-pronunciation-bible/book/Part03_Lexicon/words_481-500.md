Part: Part III — PTE High Frequency Lexicon

**Sourcing note**: same as prior batches — IPA and stress are standard,

**Batch theme**: emotions and personality traits (*anxious, anxiety,
confident, confidence, enthusiastic, enthusiasm, curious, curiosity,
ambitious, ambition, humble, humility, optimistic, optimism,
resilient, resilience, empathy, empathetic, gratitude, grateful*) — a
common Respond to a Situation / Essay / Describe Image vocabulary
domain not previously covered.

**Capstone pattern — shifting vs. stable adjective/noun pairs, side by
side**: this batch deliberately pairs each adjective with its noun (or
vice versa) to show two distinct suffix behaviors documented
throughout this lexicon:

- **Shifting pairs** (stress moves): *anxious*→*anxiety*,
  *curious*→*curiosity*, *humble*→*humility* — extending the
  stress-shift family beyond the "-tion"/"-ic"/"-ee"/"-eer" mechanisms
  already tracked, to a fourth mechanism: derivational suffixes like
  "-ety"/"-ity" pulling stress toward themselves even when attached to
  an adjective rather than a verb.
- **Three more "-ic" family shift pairs**: *enthusiasm*→
  *enthusiastic*, *optimism*→*optimistic*, *empathy*→*empathetic* —
  bringing this lexicon's "-ic" shift-pair count to seven.
- **Two more "-ity" data points**: *curiosity*, *humility* — bringing
  the "-ity" family count to eight.
- **Stable pairs** (stress does NOT move): *confident*/*confidence*
  and *ambitious*/*ambition* keep the same stressed syllable across
  both forms, and *resilient*/*resilience* is a third such pair — a
  useful negative-data-point set alongside "-ship" (Batches 9, 10)
  showing that not every derivational suffix shifts stress.

**Continuing patterns**: *anxious* and *anxiety* are a sixth and
seventh velar-nasal /ŋ/ example, after *sibling*, *upbringing*
(Batch 10), *boarding*, *onboarding*, *streaming* (Batch 11).
