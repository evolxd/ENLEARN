# Words 181–210（词汇 181–210）

Part: Part III — PTE High Frequency Lexicon
Status: Draft (Batch 7 of the planned 300–500 word target; batch size 30 words, per `README.md`)
Accent model: General American (GA), consistent with Parts II and IV

**Sourcing note**: same as Batches 1–6 — IPA and stress are standard, well-established dictionary pronunciations; this session's direct Cambridge/Oxford fetch attempts returned 403, so each entry should be individually cross-checked before being marked Done. "AI Recognition Notes" fields are `[Hypothesis]`-tagged reasoned extensions of Parts I and IV, not observed word-specific AI behavior data.

**Batch theme**: science/technology/environment vocabulary (*climate, species, ecosystem, sustainable, emission, pollution, molecule, organism, artificial intelligence, algorithm*, etc.) — a very common Read Aloud and Listening passage topic pool, plus measurement vocabulary (*quantity, quality, volume, density, velocity, capacity, efficiency*).

**New pattern — "-ity" joins the suffix-stress rule family**: Part II Ch.4 already named "-ity" alongside "-tion" and "-ic" as a stress-pulling suffix, but no lexicon entry had confirmed it with a concrete example until now. *Sustainability* and *biodiversity* are this lexicon's first "-ity" data points, following the same rule: stress lands on the syllable immediately before the suffix.

**New/continuing threads**: *algorithm* gives this lexicon's first *medial* /ð/ example (previous /θ/ and /ð/ examples were word-initial: *think*, *theory*, *hypothesis*, *thus*), worth noting since the articulatory target is identical but the surrounding context differs. *vehicle* is a second silent-letter spelling-mismatch example (silent "h"), alongside *process* (Batch 2) and *knowledge* (Batch 3). *efficiency* extends the stress-stable *efficient* (Batch 2) family. *quantity*/*quality* form a near-minimal contrast pair, both extremely high-frequency in Describe Image and Essay.

Each entry follows `book/_TEMPLATE_LEXICON_ENTRY.md`. Chinese Learner Errors cross-reference Part IV's numbered mistakes where applicable.
