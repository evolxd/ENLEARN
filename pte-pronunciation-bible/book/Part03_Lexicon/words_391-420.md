# Words 391–420（词汇 391–420）

Part: Part III — PTE High Frequency Lexicon
Status: Draft (second half of Batch 10, combined with `words_361-390.md` to form a 60-word batch — see `README.md`)
Accent model: General American (GA), consistent with Parts II and IV

**Sourcing note**: same as prior batches — IPA and stress are standard,
well-established dictionary pronunciations; this session's direct
Cambridge/Oxford fetch attempts returned 403, so each entry should be
individually cross-checked before being marked Done. "AI Recognition
Notes" fields are `[Hypothesis]`-tagged reasoned extensions of Parts I
and IV, not observed word-specific AI behavior data.

**Batch theme**: family and relationships (*household, family, relative,
sibling, parent, spouse, marriage, divorce, childhood, elderly,
guardian, upbringing, ancestor, descendant*) and health/medical care
(*diet, appetite, allergy, therapy, therapist, hospital, clinic,
patient, physician, surgeon, prescription, medication, recovery,
rehabilitation, wellness, hygiene*).

**New pattern — velar nasal /ŋ/ reduced to /n/**: *sibling* and
*upbringing* are this lexicon's first entries to flag the word-final
"-ing" velar nasal /ŋ/ as a discrete risk (rather than the "-ing"
ending generally). This is a distinct mechanism from the vowel/
consonant patterns tracked so far and is flagged as a cautious
`[Hypothesis]` extension, since it is not directly documented in this
book's Part IV sources.

**New pattern — "-cian" extends the "-tion" stress rule**: *physician*
places stress immediately before "-cian," the same position the
"-tion" rule predicts, showing the rule extends to this related
suffix spelling.

**Continuing patterns**: *family* is a fourth reduction-thread example,
joining *temperature*, *literature*, *laboratory*. *relative* is another
coda-/v/ example. *rehabilitation* is the longest "-tion" word in this
lexicon so far (six syllables), an extreme case of the equal-stress
risk. *household*, *childhood*, and *upbringing* are further compound-
noun-stress examples.

Each entry follows `book/_TEMPLATE_LEXICON_ENTRY.md`. Chinese Learner
Errors cross-reference Part IV's numbered mistakes where applicable.
