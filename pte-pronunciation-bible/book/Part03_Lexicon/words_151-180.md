# Words 151–180（词汇 151–180）

Part: Part III — PTE High Frequency Lexicon
Status: Draft (Batch 6 of the planned 300–500 word target; batch size 30 words, per `README.md`)
Accent model: General American (GA), consistent with Parts II and IV

**Sourcing note**: same as Batches 1–5 — IPA and stress are standard, well-established dictionary pronunciations; this session's direct Cambridge/Oxford fetch attempts returned 403, so each entry should be individually cross-checked before being marked Done. "AI Recognition Notes" fields are `[Hypothesis]`-tagged reasoned extensions of Parts I and IV, not observed word-specific AI behavior data.

**Batch theme**: academic/university life and workplace vocabulary (*university, institution, curriculum, assignment, lecture, seminar, graduate, degree, career, employment, workplace, management*, etc.) — high-frequency across Read Aloud, Retell Lecture, and Essay prompts, and thematically apt given this book's own test ("PTE **Academic**" — see *academic* below).

**New pattern — stress-attracting suffixes "-ee"/"-eer"**: unlike "-tion"/"-ic" (which pull stress onto the syllable immediately *before* the suffix, Part II Ch.4, reinforced across Batches 3–5), the suffixes "-ee" and "-eer" pull primary stress onto *themselves*, on the final syllable. *Employee* (stress on "-ee", contrasting with *employ*/*employer*, which keep stress on syllable 2) and *volunteer*/*career* (stress on "-eer"/final syllable) are this batch's data points. This is a second confirmed suffix-stress rule alongside "-tion"/"-ic", not a random exception.

**Continuing pattern**: *graduate* (adjective/noun vs verb) is a third data point for the adjective/verb suffix-vowel alternation tracked since Batch 4 (*appropriate*, *approximate*).

Each entry follows `book/_TEMPLATE_LEXICON_ENTRY.md`. Chinese Learner Errors cross-reference Part IV's numbered mistakes where applicable.
