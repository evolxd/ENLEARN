# Words 211–240（词汇 211–240）

Part: Part III — PTE High Frequency Lexicon
Status: Draft (second half of Batch 7, combined with `words_181-210.md` to form a 60-word batch per user direction — see `README.md`)
Accent model: General American (GA), consistent with Parts II and IV

**Sourcing note**: same as prior batches — IPA and stress are standard, well-established dictionary pronunciations; this session's direct Cambridge/Oxford fetch attempts returned 403, so each entry should be individually cross-checked before being marked Done. "AI Recognition Notes" fields are `[Hypothesis]`-tagged reasoned extensions of Parts I and IV, not observed word-specific AI behavior data.

**Batch theme**: health/medicine (*medicine, medical, treatment, symptom, diagnosis, disease, virus, vaccine, immune, nutrition, exercise*), psychology (*psychology, behavior, emotion, memory, perception, cognitive, awareness*), and history/culture (*history, historical, culture, cultural, century, ancient, civilization, heritage, tradition, custom, religion, religious*) — common Read Aloud and Retell Lecture passage topics rounding out the science/technology/environment theme from `words_181-210.md`.

**New pattern — "-ical" extends the "-ic" rule**: *historical* (stress on "-stor-", the syllable before "-ical") shows the "-ic" suffix-stress rule (Part II Ch.4) still applies when "-al" is added after "-ic" — it is the same rule, not a new one, but *history*/*historical* is this lexicon's first concrete shift example for it (history: stress on syllable 1; historical: stress shifts to syllable 2). By contrast, *medicine*/*medical*, *culture*/*cultural*, *tradition* (this batch) /*traditional* (Batch 3), and *religion*/*religious* are all stress-stable — extending the growing set of non-shift checks.

**Third silent-letter example**: *psychology* begins with a silent "p," alongside *process* (Batch 2, spelling-predicted vowel) and *knowledge*/*vehicle* (Batches 3 and 7, silent consonants).

Each entry follows `book/_TEMPLATE_LEXICON_ENTRY.md`. Chinese Learner Errors cross-reference Part IV's numbered mistakes where applicable.
