**Batch theme**: geography/urban planning (*region, rural, urban, suburb, municipal, residential, territory, boundary, coastal*) and media/technology (*media, journalism, broadcast, publication, entertainment, platform, network, digital, software, hardware, database, server, bandwidth, connectivity*).

**New pattern — compound noun stress**: English compound nouns (two nouns combined into one word) typically place primary stress on the *first* element, not the second — e.g. **NET**work, **SOFT**ware, **HARD**ware, **DATA**base, **BAND**width, and *shareholder*/*stakeholder* (companion file) all stress their first element `[Linguistics]`. This is a distinct rule from every suffix-stress pattern tracked so far ("-tion"/"-ic"/"-ity"/"-ical"/"-ial"/"-ee"/"-eer"), because it applies to compounding rather than suffixation, and it contrasts with ordinary descriptive phrases (e.g. a "green HOUSE," a house that is green, where stress is more even) versus the compound "GREENhouse" (a structure for growing plants). This distinction is well documented in general English phonetics/phonology teaching materials; a specific academic citation should be added during the Stage 7 verification pass, consistent with the sourcing caveats already flagged in Part II.

**Continuing pattern**: *bandwidth* gives this lexicon's first *word-final*-position /θ/ example (previous examples were word-initial: *think*, *theory*, *thus*; and medial: *algorithm*). *connectivity* is a fourth "-ity" rule data point, alongside *sustainability*, *biodiversity* (Batch 7), and this file continues the family.

Each entry follows `book/_TEMPLATE_LEXICON_ENTRY.md`. Chinese Learner Errors cross-reference Part IV's numbered mistakes where applicable.
