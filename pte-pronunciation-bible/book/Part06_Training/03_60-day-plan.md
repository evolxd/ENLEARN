# 3. 60-Day Plan（60天深化计划）

Part: Part VI — Training System
Status: Draft
Accent model: General American (GA), consistent with Parts II–IV

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this plan, you will be able to:

- Apply every mechanism in Part II and every mistake pattern in Part
  IV from memory, without re-reading the source chapter
- Correctly pronounce the full Part III lexicon as it exists at the
  time of this plan (Batches 1–16, ~480–500 words — see
  `book/Part03_Lexicon/README.md` for the current exact count)
- Apply Part V's strategy chapters fluently across all ten item types,
  with enough repetition that strategy application no longer requires
  conscious recall of the chapter
- Run a minimum of three full-length mock tests and demonstrate a
  measurable, logged reduction in repeated errors between the first
  and last

## Theory

Sixty days is long enough for a second full pass of the highest-value
material, not just first exposure. This plan is organized into four
two-week phases rather than 60 individually scripted days, because at
this length a rigid day-by-day script becomes brittle — life
interrupts a two-month plan more often than a two-week one. Each
phase specifies weekly targets and a repeating daily routine; you
choose which day of the week to do which task, as long as the week's
targets are met.

Four design choices distinguish this plan from the 30-Day Plan:

1. **Two full passes of Part II/IV, not one.** `[Hypothesis]` A
   mechanism drilled once and then not revisited for six weeks is
   likely to have decayed by the time Part V practice or the mock
   tests need it. Phase 3 (Weeks 5–6) is a deliberate second pass of
   Part II/IV content, timed to land roughly a month after the first.
2. **The full lexicon, not just the 300-word floor.** With 60 days,
   all sixteen batches (Batches 1–16) fit comfortably with review
   room to spare, reaching the book's 500-word ceiling rather than
   stopping at the 300-word floor as the 30-Day Plan does.
3. **Three mock tests, not two.** Three data points let you see
   whether an error is trending down, not just whether it appears
   twice. Mock tests are placed at roughly the 25%, 65%, and 95% marks
   of the plan (Days 15, 40, 58) rather than clustered at the end.
4. **A dedicated remediation week.** Phase 4 (Week 8) exists
   specifically to re-drill whatever the Day 40 mock test's error log
   shows as still-recurring, rather than assuming Phase 3's second
   pass fixed everything uniformly.

## Examples

**Daily routine template** (~50–70 minutes/day, most days):

1. **Warm-up (10–15 min).** Shadow 10–15 words drawn from across *all*
   previously covered lexicon batches, weighted toward ones flagged in
   your error log.
2. **Core task (30–45 min).** The week's assigned chapter, lexicon
   batch, or Part V item-type practice — see the phase tables below.
3. **Log (5–10 min).** Update your error log. Once a week (see each
   phase's review day), spend 20 extra minutes re-reading the Part
   II/IV chapter behind your three most frequent log entries.

A sample Week 5 (start of the second Part II/IV pass): re-read Part II
Ch.1–5 across the week's first four days, spending extra time only on
chapters tied to your Day 15 mock test error log, then use the fifth
day to re-drill the specific minimal pairs and clusters that log
identifies — not a full re-read of every chapter at equal depth.

## Exam Strategy

- The three mock tests are checkpoints, not just practice — after
  each one, spend a full day comparing its error log to the previous
  mock's before continuing new material. An error that appears in all
  three logs is your highest-priority remediation target for the
  final two weeks.
- If Week 8's remediation reveals a mechanism (not just a word list)
  still failing — e.g., a whole category like consonant clusters
  rather than one specific word — return to the relevant Part II
  chapter directly rather than continuing to drill Part III lexicon
  words that share the same underlying mechanism; fixing the
  mechanism generalizes, drilling individual words does not.
- Do not compress the two review weeks (Weeks 6 and 8) into the
  content weeks if you fall behind. If you must cut something, cut
  lexicon batch review repetitions before cutting a scheduled review
  week — the review weeks are where retention actually gets tested.

**Day 58 — Mock test #3.** Compare against both prior mocks. An error
appearing in all three logs needs a different remediation approach
than what Weeks 5–8 used — consider extending practice with the
[90-Day Plan](./04_90-day-plan.md)'s longer remediation cycle if it
persists.

**Days 59–60.** Review only. Re-read Part I Ch.5 (Common Myths). Rest.

## Summary

- Sixty days allows a full second pass of Part II/IV, the complete
  Part III lexicon as it currently stands, and three mock-test
  checkpoints instead of one or two.
- The plan is organized into four two-week phases with weekly targets,
  not individually scripted days, to tolerate schedule disruption.
- Weeks 6 and 8 are dedicated review/remediation weeks — protect them
  even under time pressure.
- An error appearing across all three mock-test logs signals a
  mechanism-level gap; fix it via the Part II/IV chapter, not more
  lexicon repetition.
