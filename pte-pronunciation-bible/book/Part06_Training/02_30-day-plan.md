# 2. 30-Day Plan（30天系统计划）

Part: Part VI — Training System
Status: Draft
Accent model: General American (GA), consistent with Parts II–IV

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this plan, you will be able to:

- Explain and apply every mechanism covered in Part II (IPA through
  incomplete plosion) and recognize its corresponding Part IV mistake
  pattern
- Correctly pronounce approximately the first 300 words of the Part
  III lexicon (Batches 1–8), covering the book's original 300-word
  minimum target
- Apply Part V's strategy chapter to every one of the ten Speaking,
  Writing, Reading, and Listening item types, with at least three
  timed practice attempts per item type
- Complete two full-length mock tests and use the error log method to
  close the gap between them

## Theory

Thirty days is enough time to cover Part II and Part IV completely
(unlike the 15-Day Plan, which triages), and to reach the lexicon's
original 300-word floor with room for repeated review — but not enough
to comfortably reach the full 500-word ceiling with the spacing needed
for durable recall. This plan is built on three principles:

1. **Sequential dependency order.** Part II must precede Part IV in
   any week, because Part IV's mistake descriptions assume Part II's
   terminology (`[Linguistics]` terms like "coda," "onset,"
   "diphthong monophthongization") is already understood — this
   mirrors the dependency Part IV's own README states. Part V depends
   on both Part I (the scoring model motivating *why* a strategy
   works) and Part II (the mechanism the strategy applies), so it is
   scheduled after both.
2. **Spaced lexicon exposure, not one-pass cramming.** `[Hypothesis]`
   Each lexicon batch is revisited at least once, roughly one week
   after first exposure, rather than seeing every word only once. This
   plan schedules light review passes in Week 3 and Week 4 for
   Week 1–2 batches specifically for this reason.
3. **Item-type practice only after mechanism is in place.** As in the
   15-Day Plan, Part V chapters are not scheduled until the relevant
   Part II/IV content is covered, so that item-type drilling reinforces
   correct articulation instead of practicing existing errors at
   higher speed.

This plan is organized into four weeks plus a two-day final review,
each with a consistent daily structure (see Exercises) rather than a
unique task per day, since 30 days of fully bespoke daily tasks becomes
hard to follow. Each week's table gives the week's chapter/lexicon
targets; the daily routine below tells you how to spend each day.

## Examples

**Daily routine template** (~75–90 minutes/day), used for every day in
this plan unless the week's table says otherwise:

1. **Warm-up (10 min).** Shadow 10 words drawn from any previously
   completed lexicon batch (skip in Week 1 before Batch 1 exists).
2. **New material (30–40 min).** Read the day's assigned chapter(s)
   from the week's table. Take notes on every `[Linguistics]` or
   `[Pearson]`-tagged claim — these are the mechanisms you'll be
   tested on, directly or indirectly.
3. **Applied practice (20–30 min).** For a Part II/IV day: drill the
   chapter's example words/minimal pairs aloud. For a Part V day:
   complete one timed practice item of that type. For a lexicon day:
   work through the assigned batch file.
4. **Log (5 min).** Add any recurring mistake to your error log, tagged
   with the Part IV chapter/mistake number it matches.

## Exam Strategy

- Do not skip the Week 3/4 review passes even if you feel confident on
  Week 1–2 material — they exist specifically to counter the recall
  decay that `[Hypothesis]` untested single-exposure vocabulary is
  expected to show under exam-day pressure.
- If a week's chapter load feels too heavy for one day, split it
  across two days rather than skipping the applied-practice step —
  reading without drilling does not change spoken output.
- Take both mock tests (Day 22 and Day 29) under real timed conditions,
  in one sitting, without pausing. Compare your Day 29 error log
  against Day 22's — recurring entries indicate a mechanism that
  needs more than one review pass; go back to the relevant Part II/IV
  chapter, not just the lexicon or Part V chapter.

## Summary

- Thirty days covers Part II and Part IV completely, all ten Part V
  item types, and the lexicon's original 300-word floor (Batches 1–8).
- Weeks are sequenced by dependency: Part II before Part IV, both
  before Part V.
- Week 3 Day 21 and Week 4 Day 28 are built-in review days — do not
  replace them with new material.
- Two full mock tests (Days 22 and 29) let you measure progress
  directly rather than relying on subjective confidence.
- For the remaining 200 lexicon words (Batches 9–16) and a deeper,
  more spaced study cycle, use the [60-Day Plan](./03_60-day-plan.md)
  or [90-Day Plan](./04_90-day-plan.md).
