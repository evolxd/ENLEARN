# 4. 90-Day Plan（90天全面掌握计划）

Part: Part VI — Training System
Status: Draft
Accent model: General American (GA), consistent with Parts II–IV

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this plan, you will be able to:

- Demonstrate mastery of every mechanism in Part II and every mistake
  pattern in Part IV across at least three spaced review cycles, not
  just two passes
- Correctly and durably pronounce the full Part III lexicon as it
  exists at the time of this plan, with evidence of retention weeks
  after first exposure, not just immediately after drilling
- Apply Part V's strategy chapters automatically, without conscious
  recall of the underlying chapter, across all ten item types
- Run a minimum of five full-length mock tests spaced across the plan
  and show a documented downward trend in repeated errors, with a
  final remediation cycle targeting whatever remains

## Theory

Ninety days is long enough to treat pronunciation training the way
skill-acquisition research treats any complex motor/cognitive skill:
as a series of spaced cycles with built-in reassessment, not a single
linear pass through material. This plan differs from the 60-Day Plan
in degree, not kind — it adds a third full review cycle and more
mock-test checkpoints, on the `[Hypothesis]` assumption that more
spaced repetitions, each separated by enough time for some forgetting
to occur, produce more durable recall than the same total study hours
compressed into fewer, longer sessions. This claim reflects general
learning-science reasoning applied by the author, not a PTE-specific
or Pearson-sourced claim — treat the specific cycle count and spacing
below as one reasonable implementation, not the only valid one.

The plan is organized into six two-week phases (12 weeks ≈ 84 days)
plus a final one-week taper (Week 13), for 90 days total. Structure:

1. **Weeks 1–2 — Foundations, first pass.** Same content as the
   60-Day Plan's Phase 1, at a slower daily pace (more time per
   chapter, since there is no need to rush toward an early mock test).
2. **Weeks 3–4 — Error corpus complete, lexicon begins (first pass).**
   Batches 1–7, roughly matching the 60-Day Plan's pace but with more
   built-in repetition per batch.
3. **Weeks 5–6 — Lexicon continues, Part V begins.** Batches 8–12,
   completing the full 500-word lexicon, plus the first four Part V
   chapters.
4. **Weeks 7–8 — Second full review cycle.** A complete re-pass of
   Part II and Part IV (not just the weakest chapters, unlike the
   60-Day Plan's targeted Phase 3) alongside Part V chapters.
5. **Weeks 9–10 — Third review cycle, mechanism-focused.** A targeted
   (not full) re-pass driven entirely by accumulated mock-test error
   logs, plus full completion of Part V.
6. **Weeks 11–12 — Intensive mock-test cycling.** Multiple full mock
   tests with remediation between each, rather than one or two
   checkpoints.
7. **Week 13 — Taper.** Reduced-intensity review only, no new
   material, mirroring the "review-only" final days used in every
   shorter plan in this Part, extended to a full week given the
   longer runway.

## Examples

**A sample third-cycle week (Week 9).** Rather than re-reading all of
Part II Ch.1–9 (as Week 7 did), Week 9 starts from the combined error
logs of mock tests #1 and #2 (see Exercises). If those logs show
recurring vowel and linking errors but no stress or rhythm errors,
Week 9 spends its chapter-review time only on Part II Ch.3
(Vowels) and Ch.8 (Linking) plus their Part IV counterparts (Ch.5 and
Ch.4), instead of mechanically re-reading all nine Part II chapters
again. This is the core difference between the second review cycle
(Weeks 7–8, comprehensive) and the third (Weeks 9–10, targeted): the
second cycle assumes everything needs reinforcement once more; the
third cycle trusts the accumulated data to say what still does.

## Exam Strategy

- Treat each of the five mock tests as a checkpoint, not a
  pass/fail event. Compare each new log against *all* prior logs, not
  just the most recent one — an error present in mocks #1, #3, and #5
  but absent from #2 and #4 is not "fixed," it is intermittent, and
  intermittent errors are exactly what a fatigue-inducing real exam
  is likely to surface.
- If, by mock test #4 (end of Week 11), an error from Week 1–2's
  material is still recurring, do not wait for Week 13 — pause and
  address it immediately with a focused one-day session against the
  original Part II/IV chapter. A 90-day plan has enough slack to
  absorb this without abandoning the schedule.
- Do not let Week 13's taper become a sixth content week under
  pressure to "fit in more." `[Hypothesis]` The taper's purpose is
  consolidation and reduced cognitive load heading into the exam,
  mirroring the shorter plans' final review-only days; treat it as
  protected time, the same way Weeks 6 and 8 are protected in the
  60-Day Plan.

## Summary

- Ninety days allows three distinct review cycles (comprehensive,
  then targeted, then intensive mock-cycling) instead of the 60-Day
  Plan's two, plus five mock-test checkpoints instead of three.
- The third review cycle (Weeks 9–10) is data-driven, not a
  mechanical re-read — it targets only what prior mock-test error
  logs show as unresolved.
- Compare every new mock-test log against *all* prior logs, not just
  the most recent, to catch intermittent (not just persistent) errors.
- Week 13 is a protected taper week — do not convert it into
  additional content time even under pressure to cover more.
