# 1. 15-Day Plan（15天冲刺计划）

Part: Part VI — Training System
Status: Draft
Accent model: General American (GA), consistent with Parts II–IV

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this plan, you will be able to:

- Name the three PTE Speaking enabling skills (Content, Oral Fluency,
  Pronunciation) and state which chapters address each
- Correctly articulate the five highest-frequency Mandarin-to-English
  error patterns catalogued in Part IV Ch.1
- Apply Part V's item-specific strategy to all ten Speaking, Writing,
  Reading, and Listening item types under timed conditions
- Recognize and correctly pronounce the first 150–210 words of the
  Part III lexicon (Batches 1–5, plus part of Batch 6)
- Complete one full-length mock test simulation before exam day

## Theory

This plan assumes you have an exam booked within roughly two weeks and
cannot cover the full book. Fifteen days is not enough time to read
every chapter and drill all ~500 lexicon words with the repetition
needed for durable recall, so this plan makes deliberate triage
choices rather than attempting shallow coverage of everything.

Three triage principles govern the day-by-day schedule below:

1. **Mechanism before item type.** Days 1–7 cover Part II's phonetic
   foundations and Part IV's error corpus before any Part V item-type
   practice begins. `[Pearson]` Pronunciation and Oral Fluency are
   scored on every spoken response type (see Part I Ch.1), so a fix
   made once in Part II pays off across Read Aloud, Repeat Sentence,
   Describe Image, Retell Lecture, and Respond to a Situation
   simultaneously. Practicing item-type strategy before fixing the
   underlying articulation wastes repetitions on the wrong target.
2. **Highest-frequency errors first.** Part IV ranks its mistakes by
   how often they are documented (see each Part IV chapter's ordering).
   With limited days, this plan follows that ranking rather than
   covering every mistake with equal time.
3. **A word learned once is not learned.** `[Hypothesis]` Single-pass
   exposure to a new word is unlikely to produce reliable recall under
   exam pressure. Rather than front-loading all lexicon batches, this
   plan spreads five to six batches across the full 15 days and
   revisits each previously covered batch briefly on later days
   (see the "Daily Warm-Up" note in Exercises), instead of drilling
   each batch once and moving on.

Given this triage, several things are deliberately **not** attempted in
15 days: batches 6 (partial) through 12 of the Part III lexicon,
and a second full pass of Part II. If your exam is more than three
weeks away, use the [30-Day Plan](./02_30-day-plan.md) instead — it
covers the same material with room for full lexicon coverage.

## Examples

A worked example of Day 4 (Word Stress + Lexicon Batch 1), showing how
the daily structure combines new material with review:

1. **Warm-up (10 min).** Shadow 10 words from Day 1's material (none
   yet at Day 4 — skip on days with no prior lexicon batch).
2. **New theory (20 min).** Read Part II Ch.4 (Word Stress). Note the
   `[Linguistics]` claim that English stress is lexical (memorized per
   word) rather than rule-predicted from spelling alone.
3. **Error-corpus cross-reference (15 min).** Read Part IV Ch.2 (Top
   Stress Mistakes). Identify which of its numbered mistakes apply to
   words you personally mis-stress — keep a running list (see Exam
   Strategy below).
4. **New lexicon batch (30 min).** Work through Part III
   `words_001-030.md`. For each word: read the IPA, say it aloud
   against the audio model in your source dictionary (see
   `REFERENCES.md`), then use it in the example sentence provided.
5. **Log (5 min).** Write down 3 words or patterns that felt hardest.
   These feed into Day 15's targeted review.

## Exam Strategy

- If you miss a day, do not try to double up the next day. Cut the
  lexicon batch for that day in half and move the rest to a spare slot
  (Day 15 has no new material scheduled, so it can absorb overflow —
  but only lexicon, not skipped chapters; a skipped Part II or Part V
  chapter should be read even if late, since later days depend on it).
- Keep a running **error log**: every time you notice a mistake
  (either self-caught or from a practice recording), write down which
  Part IV chapter and numbered mistake it matches. Review this log for
  10 minutes before each mock/practice session — this targets your
  actual errors instead of generic drilling.
- Do not introduce new lexicon words on Day 15. `[Hypothesis]` Reviewing
  only previously seen material in the final 24–48 hours before a
  timed exam is a common test-preparation practice for reducing
  cognitive load on exam day; this plan treats it as prudent, not as a
  proven scoring mechanism.

## Exercises

**Daily Warm-Up** (every day from Day 5 onward, 10 minutes before new
material): shadow 8–10 words drawn at random from *all* previously
covered lexicon batches, not just the most recent one. This is the
plan's only built-in spaced-repetition mechanism given the short
timeframe — treat it as non-optional.

## Summary

- Fifteen days cannot cover the full book — this plan triages toward
  mechanism (Part II/IV) before item-type strategy (Part V), and
  toward the first ~150–210 lexicon words rather than all ~500.
- The Daily Warm-Up (Days 5–15) is the plan's spaced-repetition
  mechanism — do not skip it even when short on time.
- Keep a running error log tied to Part IV's numbered mistakes; use it
  to target review instead of drilling generically.
- Day 15 is deliberately review-only, with no new lexicon or theory.
- If your exam is more than three weeks out, use the
  [30-Day Plan](./02_30-day-plan.md) instead for fuller coverage.
