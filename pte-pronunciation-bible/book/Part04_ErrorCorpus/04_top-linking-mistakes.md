# 4. Top Linking Mistakes（连读高频错误）

Part: Part IV — Chinese Error Corpus
Status: Draft
Accent model: General American (GA), consistent with Part II
L1 scope: Mandarin Chinese (普通话)

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

- Explain why the segmental patterns in Ch.1 (final-consonant deletion, epenthesis) directly interfere with the linking mechanisms in Part II Ch.8.
- Identify word-boundary pausing and missing consonant-to-vowel linking as the two dominant linking error types.
- Distinguish an intentional pause (acceptable, Part II Ch.3) from a habitual word-by-word break (a documented error pattern).
- Apply targeted linking drills that address the Ch.1 interaction directly.

## Theory

### Key Terms 关键术语

This chapter reuses linking terminology defined in Part II Ch.8 (Linking) — consonant-to-vowel linking, linking /r/, intrusive glide — rather than redefining it.

### Why linking errors are downstream of Ch.1's segmental patterns

Linking (Part II Ch.8) depends on a word-final consonant being available to carry forward into the next word's vowel. If that final consonant has already been deleted or altered by epenthesis (Ch.1 of this Part), there is nothing left to link — the error is not really "a linking mistake" in isolation; it is Ch.1's segmental pattern breaking a downstream mechanism. This chapter treats linking errors as this specific interaction, not as an unrelated third phenomenon.

### Mistake 1 — Word-by-word pausing instead of linking

Research on Chinese-background ESL learners' production of English connected speech processes documents systematic difficulty achieving native-like linking and connected-speech patterns, consistent with a broader tendency to produce words as more separated units rather than a continuous stream `[Practice]`. This connects directly to the syllable-timed rhythm pattern documented in Ch.3 of this Part: if each word is produced as a similarly weighted, separated unit, the natural pressure to link across word boundaries (which arises from rhythmic compression, Part II Ch.5–6) is reduced.

### Mistake 2 — Missing consonant-to-vowel linking due to final-consonant deletion

Where Ch.1's Mistake 3 (final consonant deletion) applies, linking cannot occur at all — there is no consonant left to carry forward. For example, if *"turn it off"* is produced with the final consonants of *turn* and *it* already dropped or vowel-inserted ("tur-nuh it-uh off" pattern), the native linked form /tɜːr nɪ tɒf/ (Part II Ch.8) is structurally unreachable, regardless of whether the learner has separately studied linking rules. This is why this chapter is sequenced after Ch.1: linking practice is unlikely to succeed until the underlying final-consonant pattern is addressed.

### Mistake 3 — Missing vowel-to-vowel glide insertion

Independent of Mistakes 1–2, some learners do not insert the intrusive /j/ or /w/ glide at vowel-to-vowel word boundaries (Part II Ch.8), instead producing a glottal stop or a brief silence between the two vowels (e.g. *"go on"* produced as /ɡoʊ ʔɒn/ rather than /ɡoʊ wɒn/) `[Practice]`. This is a more independent pattern than Mistakes 1–2, since it is not caused by a missing consonant, but by not yet having acquired the specific glide-insertion rule.

## Exam Strategy

- Address Ch.1's final-consonant patterns before drilling linking in isolation — this chapter's Mistake 2 shows that linking practice without that foundation has a low ceiling.
- During preparation time for Read Aloud/Repeat Sentence, mark likely linking points (consonant-final word before vowel-initial word) and rehearse the linked form directly, rather than relying on it to happen automatically.
- For Listening and Repeat Sentence input, expect native audio to use these linked forms as the default (Part II Ch.6) — do not mistake a linked phrase for an unfamiliar word or missing content.

## Exercises

1. Take the three example phrases above and record yourself producing them. Identify which mistake (1, 2, or 3) is present, if any.
2. Choose five phrases from your own Read Aloud practice material with a consonant-final word followed by a vowel-initial word. Practice the linked form explicitly, checking that the final consonant is not deleted first.
3. Practice the vowel-to-vowel pairs from Part II Ch.8 (*"I am"*, *"go on"*) checking specifically for a glottal stop versus the correct intrusive glide.

## Summary

- Many apparent "linking mistakes" are downstream consequences of Ch.1's segmental final-consonant patterns, not an independent error category.
- Mistake 1 (word-by-word pausing) reflects the same syllable-timed tendency documented in Ch.3.
- Mistake 2 (missing consonant-to-vowel linking) occurs specifically when the linking consonant has already been deleted or altered.
- Mistake 3 (missing glide insertion) is a more independent pattern, addressable through direct rule instruction (Part II Ch.8).
- Effective linking correction requires first stabilizing final-consonant production (Ch.1), then applying Part II Ch.8's specific linking rules.
