# 3. Vowels（元音）

Part: Part II — Pronunciation Foundations
Status: Draft
Accent model: General American (GA). Vowel inventory and rhotic vowels (/ɝ/, /ɚ/) are specific to GA; RP handles some of these differently (noted where relevant).

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

- List the GA monophthongs and diphthongs with example words and IPA symbols.
- Explain the tense/lax vowel distinction and why it matters for English but has no direct equivalent in Mandarin.
- Identify the GA rhotic vowels /ɝ/ and /ɚ/ and where they occur.
- Predict common Mandarin-L1 vowel confusions from the mismatch between English's vowel inventory and Mandarin's.

## Theory

### Key Terms 关键术语

| English | 中文 | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| Monophthong | 单元音 | A vowel with a single, steady articulation throughout its production. |
| Diphthong | 双元音 | A vowel that glides from one vowel quality to another within a single syllable. |
| Tense/Lax Vowel | 紧元音／松元音 | A pairing of vowels distinguished by muscular tension and typically length (e.g. /iː/ tense vs /ɪ/ lax). |
| Vowel Length | 元音长度 | The relative duration of a vowel, marked with ː in IPA for longer vowels. |
| Rhotic Vowel | R 化元音 | A vowel pronounced with r-coloring, written /ɝ/ (stressed) or /ɚ/ (unstressed) in GA. |

### The GA vowel inventory

General American is commonly described with the following vowels `[Linguistics]`. Exact counts vary slightly across sources depending on dialect and how rhotic vowels and mergers (such as the cot–caught merger in many GA speakers) are treated — the inventory below reflects a standard teaching model, not a single universally agreed count `[Linguistics]`.

**Monophthongs:**

| IPA | Example word | Tense/Lax |
|---|---|---|
| /iː/ | fleece | Tense |
| /ɪ/ | kit | Lax |
| /eɪ/* | face | (diphthong, see below) |
| /ɛ/ | dress | Lax |
| /æ/ | trap | Lax |
| /ɑː/ | father/lot | Tense |
| /ɔː/ | thought | Tense |
| /ʊ/ | foot | Lax |
| /uː/ | goose | Tense |
| /ʌ/ | strut | Lax |
| /ə/ | about (unstressed) | Lax (reduced) |
| /ɝ/ | nurse (stressed) | Rhotic |
| /ɚ/ | letter (unstressed) | Rhotic |

**Diphthongs:**

### Tense vs lax: why this pairing matters

Several English vowels form tense/lax pairs distinguished by both vowel quality and length: /iː/–/ɪ/ (*sheep*/*ship*), /uː/–/ʊ/ (*fool*/*full*) `[Linguistics]`. Mandarin's vowel system does not organize contrasts this way `[Linguistics]`, so Mandarin L1 learners frequently merge these pairs, producing near-homophones that are distinguished in English but not for them — this can create actual Content-scoring risk (Part I Ch.4) when the merged pair happens to be two different real words, not just a Pronunciation-band issue.

### Rhotic vowels are a GA-specific feature

/ɝ/ and /ɚ/ (r-colored vowels, as in *nurse* and *letter*) are a defining feature of GA's rhotic system — GA pronounces /r/ wherever it is spelled, including after vowels, while RP (non-rhotic) generally does not `[Linguistics]`. This book's GA default means PTE vocabulary entries in Part III will show these r-colored vowels; if you are used to RP-style dictionaries, expect this difference and do not treat it as an error in either system.

## Examples

| Minimal pair | IPA contrast | Risk if merged |
|---|---|---|
| sheep / ship | /iː/ vs /ɪ/ | Content error: different words |
| fool / full | /uː/ vs /ʊ/ | Content error: different words |
| bad / bed | /æ/ vs /ɛ/ | Content error: different words |
| cart / cut | /ɑː/ vs /ʌ/ | Content error: different words |

## Exam Strategy

- Treat tense/lax minimal pairs as high-value practice targets, since a merged vowel can change a Read Aloud/Repeat Sentence response into a different word — a Content error, not just a Pronunciation-band issue (Part I Ch.4).
- When checking a new word's IPA (Part III), note whether it contains a vowel from a tense/lax pair you have not yet drilled, and prioritize accordingly.
- Do not assume vowel length is optional — length is part of what distinguishes many of these pairs, alongside quality.

## Exercises

1. Record yourself producing the four minimal pairs in the Examples table above. Ask a listener (or compare to a reference recording) whether each pair sounds clearly distinct.
2. Identify three English words in your own vocabulary that contain a rhotic vowel (/ɝ/ or /ɚ/), and check their IPA in a GA-based dictionary entry.
3. Take a short paragraph and mark every diphthong you can identify (using the five listed above) before reading it aloud, then check whether your recorded vowel glides match the target diphthongs.

## Summary

- GA has roughly 12 monophthongs (including two rhotic vowels) and 5 diphthongs, though exact counts vary by source and dialect `[Linguistics]`.
- Tense/lax vowel pairs (e.g. /iː/–/ɪ/, /uː/–/ʊ/) are distinctive in English but have no direct equivalent contrast system in Mandarin, making them a predictable confusion point `[Linguistics]`.
- Rhotic vowels /ɝ/ and /ɚ/ are specific to GA's rhotic system and will differ from RP-based dictionary entries.
- Merged vowel pairs can cause actual Content errors (different words), not just Pronunciation-band reductions — connecting this chapter directly to Part I Ch.4.
