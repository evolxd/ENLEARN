### Format and scoring recap

Write Email presents a scenario with a small number of required points (commonly reported as three bullet points) that the response must address, within a reported 9-minute time limit and a word range of roughly 50–120 words (a 100-word target commonly recommended) `[Practice]` (secondary-sourced; re-verify before publication, including the module caution above). Reported scoring dimensions: Content (coverage of all required bullet points — missing even one is reported to significantly reduce the score), Form (email structure: greeting and closing present, appropriate length), Tone (formality matched to the situation), Grammar, Vocabulary, Spelling, and Organization (logical paragraphing) `[Practice]`.

### Covering every required point

Because missing even one of the required bullet points is reported to significantly reduce the Content score, the highest-priority strategy is straightforward: identify every required point from the prompt explicitly (e.g. by listing them before drafting) and confirm each appears in the final response, rather than trusting memory or a general sense of having "covered the topic."

### Matching tone and structure to the scenario

An email response is expected to include a greeting and closing appropriate to the scenario's formality level (e.g. a workplace complaint vs a casual request to a friend), and to organize its points into clear paragraphs rather than a single undifferentiated block `[Practice]`.

## Examples

Prompt scenario (paraphrased): a customer writing to a company about a delayed order, expected to (1) describe the problem, (2) explain the impact, (3) request a specific resolution.

An effective structure: greeting → one paragraph describing the delay and its impact → one paragraph requesting a specific resolution → closing. Each of the three required points maps to a specific, identifiable part of the response.

## Exam Strategy

- List the required bullet points explicitly before drafting, and check each is addressed before finalizing.
- Match formality to the scenario — a professional complaint and a casual personal email call for different openings, closings, and vocabulary.
- Organize distinct points into separate paragraphs rather than one continuous block.
