## Theory

### Key Terms 关键术语

| English | 中文 | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| Form (SWT sense) | 格式要求 | The requirement that the response be exactly one grammatically complete sentence within a specified word range. |

### Format and scoring recap

Summarize Written Text requires a single, complete sentence between roughly 5 and 75 words summarizing a source passage, within a 10-minute time limit; a commonly recommended target length is around 35–55 words `[Practice]` (secondary-sourced; re-verify before publication). Scoring covers Content (coverage of the main idea and key supporting points), Form, Grammar, Vocabulary, and Spelling `[Practice]`.

### The Form rule is a hard constraint, not a style preference

Test-prep sources consistently report that writing two or more sentences — even if each is grammatically correct — scores zero on Form, which directly caps the overall task score regardless of how well Content, Grammar, and Vocabulary are otherwise handled `[Practice]`. This is a binary structural rule, not a matter of degree: a response either is one sentence within the word range, or it is not.

### One-sentence structure

A reliable structure for satisfying Form while covering Content is to combine the source's main idea with its most important supporting point(s) using subordination (e.g. relative clauses, participial phrases, or coordinating conjunctions joined within a single sentence) rather than writing separate sentences and hoping to merge them after the fact.

## Examples

Source passage main idea: a study found remote work increases reported employee satisfaction but may reduce cross-team collaboration.

One-sentence summary: *"A recent study found that while remote work has increased employees' reported satisfaction, it may also be reducing collaboration between different teams, raising questions about how organizations should balance flexibility with cross-team communication."*

## Exam Strategy

- Draft mentally (or on scratch paper) as a single sentence from the start, rather than writing multiple sentences and attempting to merge them under time pressure.
- Prioritize the source's main idea plus its most significant supporting point; do not attempt to include every detail from the source passage.
- Check word count and sentence count before finalizing — a single grammatical error is recoverable in Grammar scoring, but a Form violation is not.

## Exercises

1. Take a short article and draft a single-sentence summary within the 35–55 word target range.
2. Practice combining two separate ideas into one sentence using subordination, without exceeding a natural sentence structure.
3. Review a draft summary and count both words and sentences before considering it final.

## Summary

- Summarize Written Text requires exactly one sentence within roughly 5–75 words; violating this caps the score regardless of content quality.
- Scoring covers Content, Form, Grammar, Vocabulary, and Spelling.
- A reliable strategy is drafting as a single sentence from the start using subordination, not writing multiple sentences and merging them.
