## Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

- State Essay's word count, time limit, and reported scoring dimensions.
- Apply a structure that supports content relevance, coherence, and logical argumentation within the time limit.
- Allocate the 20-minute window across planning, writing, and review.

## Theory

### Key Terms 关键术语

| English | 中文 | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| Coherence | 连贯性 | Whether ideas connect logically from sentence to sentence and paragraph to paragraph. |
| Essay Structure | 议论文结构 | The organizational pattern (e.g. introduction, body paragraphs, conclusion) expected of a formal argumentative essay. |

### Format and scoring recap

Essay requires a response of roughly 200–300 words to a given prompt, within a 20-minute window for planning, writing, and review `[Practice]` (secondary-sourced; re-verify before publication). Reported scoring dimensions: content relevance, coherence, grammar, vocabulary, and essay structure `[Practice]`; the task is described as testing the ability to organize ideas, argue logically, and write clearly.

### Time allocation within the 20-minute window

Because the window covers planning, writing, and review together, a deliberate allocation (e.g. roughly 2–3 minutes planning, 13–15 minutes writing, 2–3 minutes review) is more reliable than starting to write immediately without a plan or spending most of the time writing with no review pass — an unreviewed essay risks avoidable grammar and spelling errors that a brief final check would catch.

### A structure that supports coherence

A conventional structure — introduction stating a clear position, 2–3 body paragraphs each developing one supporting point, and a conclusion restating the position — supports the coherence and structure dimensions directly, because it gives each paragraph a single, identifiable function rather than mixing multiple ideas together.

## Examples

Prompt (paraphrased): *"Some people believe technology has made communication less personal. To what extent do you agree?"*

Structure: introduction (state a position — e.g. partial agreement), body paragraph 1 (a way technology has reduced personal connection, with a specific example), body paragraph 2 (a countervailing way technology has enabled personal connection, maintaining the "partial agreement" position), conclusion (restate the nuanced position).

## Exam Strategy

- Spend a short, deliberate planning phase identifying your position and 2–3 supporting points before writing, rather than discovering your argument as you write.
- Keep each body paragraph focused on one point to support coherence scoring.
- Reserve time at the end for a review pass focused on grammar and spelling, since these are scored separately from content and structure.
- Stay within the word range — significantly under or over the target range is a structural risk independent of content quality, mirroring the Form-constraint logic in Part V Ch.6.

## Exercises

1. Practice a full 20-minute essay with explicit time-boxing (planning/writing/review) and note whether you actually completed all three phases.
2. Take a completed essay draft and check whether each body paragraph develops exactly one point, or whether points are mixed across paragraphs.
3. Review a past essay draft specifically for grammar and spelling only, separate from a content/structure review pass.

## Summary

- Essay requires roughly 200–300 words within 20 minutes, scored on content relevance, coherence, grammar, vocabulary, and structure.
- Deliberate time allocation across planning, writing, and review is more reliable than writing without a plan or skipping review.
- A conventional introduction/body/conclusion structure, with one point per body paragraph, directly supports the coherence and structure scoring dimensions.
